# Sampayoga Reference Sheet

## What Association Means

A citta never arises alone. Every act of knowing comes with a retinue of cetasikas that arise with it, and the relation between them is called sampayoga — association, literally "yoking together." The eighty-nine cittas and the fifty-two cetasikas are not two populations that meet; they are two ways of describing one event.

Four relations define the yoking. They are stated as a fixed formula and each of them is strict.

| Relation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Ekuppāda | Arising together |
| Ekanirodha | Ceasing together |
| Ekālambana | Having the same object |
| Ekavatthuka | Having the same base |

**Ekuppāda — arising together.** The citta and its cetasikas come into being at the same moment, not in sequence. Contact does not precede feeling by an interval, and intention does not follow perception; the whole assembly arises at once. Where the texts describe one factor as conditioning another, the conditioning is simultaneous, not temporal.

**Ekanirodha — ceasing together.** They perish together as well, in the same submoment of dissolution. No cetasika outlives the citta it arose with, and none carries over into the next. What looks like a mood persisting across minutes is a rapid series of similar assemblies, each complete and each destroyed.

**Ekālambana — same object.** All the factors in one citta take exactly one object, the same one the citta takes. Contact meets that object, feeling feels it, perception marks it, intention is directed at it. This single constraint does most of the work in the exclusivity rules below, since two factors that require different objects can never be found in one citta.

**Ekavatthuka — same base.** Where there is a physical base, citta and cetasikas depend on the same one — eye-consciousness and its seven factors all rest on the eye-sensitivity, not on separate sites. In the immaterial realm there is no material base at all, so this fourth relation simply does not apply there, while the first three still hold.

The conclusion these four are meant to force is that a citta and its cetasikas are one indivisible event. There is no consciousness that first exists and then acquires attachments, no bare awareness underneath the greed to which the greed is added. A moment of greedy consciousness is not awareness plus greed; it is a single arising whose character is greedy throughout, and it is gone before anything can be subtracted from it.

## The Two Directions of Analysis

The same territory is surveyed twice, from opposite ends. The tradition teaches both because either one alone gives a distorted picture of what a mind-moment is.

**Sampayoga-naya — the method of association.** Take one cetasika and ask: in how many of the eighty-nine cittas does it arise, and which ones? This is the factor's range. It answers questions like how far wisdom can reach and how narrowly conceit is confined.

**Saṅgaha-naya — the method of inclusion.** Take one citta and ask: how many cetasikas compose it, and which ones? This is the citta's composition. It answers what a given moment actually contains, which is what determines its power, its moral weight, and what it can do.

Neither method is derivable from the other by inspection. Sampayoga tells you that wisdom occurs in forty-seven cittas but not what else is in them; saṅgaha tells you that the first great wholesome citta has thirty-eight factors but not how rare or common each of those factors is elsewhere. Read together they give the two things a practitioner needs: which states are within reach, and what a state is made of once reached.

## Sampayoga — Factor by Factor

Counts below are on the eighty-nine-citta scheme unless noted. Three of them — vitakka, vicāra, and pīti — are traditionally computed with the eight supramundane cittas reckoned jhāna-wise, and that is flagged where it matters.

### 1. Sabbacittasādhāraṇa — The seven universals

Contact, feeling, perception, intention, one-pointedness, life faculty, and attention arise in all eighty-nine cittas without exception. They are the minimum structure of a knowing event: something met, felt, marked, aimed at, unified, sustained, and turned toward. Remove any one of them and there is no cognition left to speak of.

| Factor | Cittas | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phassa, vedanā, saññā, cetanā, ekaggatā, jīvitindriya, manasikāra | 89 | All |

### 2. Pakiṇṇaka — The six occasionals

These arise only in some cittas, and their absences are informative. Each exclusion below has a reason in the nature of the factor itself.

| Factor | Cittas | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vitakka | 55 | 44 sense-sphere (all 54 except the ten sense consciousnesses) + 11 first-jhāna cittas |
| Vicāra | 66 | The above 55 + 11 second-jhāna cittas |
| Adhimokkha | 78 | All except the ten sense consciousnesses and the doubt-accompanied citta |
| Viriya | 73 | All except sixteen rootless cittas (all eighteen but for mind-door adverting and the smile-producer) |
| Pīti | 51 | 18 sense-sphere cittas with joy + 33 first-, second-, and third-jhāna cittas |
| Chanda | 69 | All except the eighteen rootless and the two delusion-rooted |

Vitakka's 55 counts the eleven first-jhāna cittas as three fine-material and eight supramundane. Vicāra's 66 and pīti's 51 require the supramundane to be counted jhāna-wise throughout — the eleven second-jhāna cittas and the thirty-three cittas of the first three jhānas both include eight supramundane at each level. Computed strictly on the eighty-nine scheme, where all supramundane cittas are treated as first-jhāna, vicāra and pīti would come out lower; the traditional figures are the jhāna-wise ones and are given here as the tradition gives them.

The exclusions run as follows. The ten sense consciousnesses lack vitakka and adhimokkha because they neither apply the mind to an object nor decide anything about it — they merely receive. Adhimokkha is absent from the doubt-rooted citta because deciding and wavering are contradictory. Viriya is absent from sixteen rootless cittas because passive reception exerts no effort. Pīti is absent wherever feeling is not joyful, and from the fourth jhāna besides, which keeps joyful feeling but has let rapture go. Chanda, the wish to act, is absent from the rootless because they undertake nothing, and from the delusion-rooted because confusion has no aim.

### 3. Akusala — The fourteen unwholesome

| Factor | Cittas | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moha, ahirika, anottappa, uddhacca | 12 | All unwholesome cittas |
| Lobha | 8 | The greed-rooted |
| Diṭṭhi | 4 | Greed-rooted with wrong view |
| Māna | 4 | Greed-rooted without wrong view |
| Dosa, issā, macchariya, kukkucca | 2 | The hatred-rooted |
| Thīna, middha | 5 | The prompted unwholesome: 4 greed-rooted, 1 hatred-rooted |
| Vicikicchā | 1 | The doubt-accompanied delusion-rooted citta |

The four in the first row are the akusala universals, and their presence in all twelve is what makes an unwholesome citta unwholesome. Delusion is the constant, which is why the system has no greed without confusion and no hatred without it either. Sloth and torpor are confined to prompted cittas because a limp mind is precisely one that needed inducement to act at all.

### 4. Sobhana — The twenty-five beautiful

| Factor | Cittas | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The 19 universal beautiful factors | 59 | All beautiful cittas: 24 sense-sphere + 15 fine-material + 12 immaterial + 8 supramundane |
| Sammā vācā, sammā kammanta, sammā ājīva | 16 | 8 great wholesome + 8 supramundane |
| Karuṇā, muditā | 28 | 8 great wholesome + 8 great functional + 12 fine-material of the first four jhānas |
| Paññindriya | 47 | 12 sense-sphere beautiful with knowledge + 15 fine-material + 12 immaterial + 8 supramundane |

The nineteen universal beautiful factors — confidence, mindfulness, shame, dread, non-greed, non-hatred, equanimity, and the six pairs — are to the wholesome side what the four akusala universals are to the unwholesome. Every beautiful citta has all nineteen, which is why there is no wholesome moment without mindfulness in it. The abstinences, illimitables, and wisdom are the variable remainder.

On the one-hundred-twenty-one-citta scheme every count that includes all eight supramundane cittas rises by thirty-two, since those eight become forty. The nineteen beautiful universals then occur in 91, the three abstinences in 48, and wisdom in 79. Karuṇā and muditā stay at 28, since they never occur in the supramundane at all.

The pattern is legible once the deltas are seen: neutral feeling costs pīti, prompting adds sloth and torpor, and the hatred-rooted totals are inflated by counting envy, stinginess, and worry, though only one of them can be present at a time. The two delusion-rooted cittas are the thinnest unwholesome states in the system, lacking chanda entirely and, in the doubt-accompanied one, adhimokkha as well.

### 2. Ahetuka citta — Rootless

| Citta | Cetasikas |
| --- | --- |
| The ten sense consciousnesses | 7 |
| Receiving (2), investigating with neutral feeling (2), five-door adverting | 10 |
| Investigating with joy | 11 |
| Mind-door adverting | 11 |
| Smile-producing | 12 |

The ten sense consciousnesses have the seven universals and nothing else, and this is the most important single figure in the saṅgaha method. Bare seeing is astonishingly thin: no application of mind to the object, no decision about it, no energy, no interest, no wish. Everything that seems to belong to seeing — the recognition of what is seen, the liking or disliking, the story about it — belongs to later cittas in the process, and the moment of seeing itself contains none of it.

Thirty-eight is the maximum any citta reaches, and it belongs to the first great wholesome citta: thirteen aññasamāna, nineteen beautiful universals, three abstinences, two illimitables, and wisdom. The resultants fall to a ceiling of thirty-three because they contain neither abstinences nor illimitables — a sense-sphere result abstains from nothing and takes the object handed to it by past kamma rather than a being. The functional cittas of the arahant keep the illimitables but lose the abstinences, since for one in whom transgression can no longer arise there is no occasion of refraining.

The descent from thirty-five is the shedding of jhāna factors: vitakka at the second, vicāra at the third, pīti at the fourth. The drop of two at the fifth is different in kind — compassion and appreciative joy fall away, because those two require the feeling-tone of the lower jhānas and the fifth is grounded in equanimity. The immaterial cittas match the fifth jhāna exactly, differing from it only in object.

On the eighty-nine-citta scheme, where the eight supramundane cittas are reckoned as first-jhāna, each has thirty-six. On the one-hundred-twenty-one-citta scheme they are distributed across the five levels and the figures above apply. Thirty-six is two short of the mundane maximum of thirty-eight, and the two missing are compassion and appreciative joy, since the object is Nibbāna and not a suffering or happy being. Everything else that the first great wholesome citta can contain is here and is fixed rather than optional: wisdom is invariable, and so are all three abstinences.

## Exclusivity and Co-Arising Rules

**Greed and hatred never co-arise, though delusion accompanies both.** Lobha adheres to its object and dosa strikes against it, and one citta with one object cannot be doing both at once; the feeling settles it too, since hatred is invariably accompanied by displeasure and greed never is. What people call love-hate is an alternation, rapid enough to feel simultaneous. Delusion is under no such constraint, because it is not a stance toward the object but a failure to see it, and that failure is what permits both of the others — hence moha in all twelve unwholesome cittas and lobha in only eight, dosa in only two.

**Diṭṭhi and māna never co-arise.** Both are forms of self-appropriation, but they operate differently: wrong view construes the object as a self, settling the matter dogmatically, while conceit measures a presumed self against others as better, equal, or worse. The commentaries say two lions do not share one den. The consequence is structural: since view occupies four of the eight greed-rooted cittas, conceit can only appear in the other four, the view-dissociated ones — and even there it is unfixed, so most greedy moments contain no conceit at all.

**Issā, macchariya and kukkucca arise singly and never together.** Each requires a different object: envy takes another's success, stinginess takes one's own possession or standing, and worry takes one's own past deed. Ekālambana forbids a single citta from holding three objects, so although all three are listed among the factors of the hatred-rooted cittas, at most one is present in any given moment. This is why the hatred-rooted totals of twenty and twenty-two overstate what is actually there.

**The doubt-accompanied citta lacks adhimokkha entirely.** Determination commits to the object and doubt refuses to; they are not merely opposed in tendency but mutually exclusive in function, so no citta can contain both. This is the only citta outside the sense consciousnesses from which adhimokkha is absent, and its absence is what gives doubt its peculiar paralysis. Even one-pointedness, though present as a universal, is too weak here to hold the object steady.

**Pīti requires somanassa but is not guaranteed by it.** Rapture is the mind's delight and interest in its object, and delight is only intelligible where the affective tone is joyful. So pīti drops out of every citta with equanimity, out of both hatred-rooted cittas with their displeasure, and out of body-consciousness whether painful or pleasant — bodily sukha is not somanassa, and pīti is tied to the mental feeling, not the bodily one. That accounts for the fifth jhāna and the immaterial attainments as well, which are grounded in equanimity.

The dependence runs one way only. Sixty-two cittas are accompanied by somanassa and pīti arises in fifty-one of them; the eleven that are left out are the fourth-jhāna cittas, which retain joyful feeling and still have no rapture. This is the clearest evidence in the system that the two are distinct factors rather than one thing named twice. Pīti is the mind's excitement at getting the object, an agitation that still has something of reaching in it; sukha is the contentment of resting in what has been got. The fourth jhāna sheds the first while keeping the second, and only at the fifth does the feeling itself become equanimous. The stock simile makes the difference: pīti is the traveller's elation at sighting water, sukha is the ease of drinking it.

**The three virati arise one at a time in mundane wholesome consciousness, all three at once in every supramundane consciousness.** Abstention is an event with an occasion: at the moment one declines a lie, right speech arises; at the moment one declines a theft, right action does. The occasions are distinct, so only one abstinence can occupy a mundane wholesome citta, and often none does, since most wholesome moments involve no refraining at all. In path and fruition all three are present together and fixed. In the path they eradicate the capacity for transgression, accomplished in a single moment on a single object. In fruition they are the purity of speech, action, and livelihood that the path has already accomplished.

**Karuṇā and muditā never co-arise, and neither occurs in the supramundane.** Compassion takes a suffering being as its object and appreciative joy takes a happy one, and no being can be presented to a single citta under both descriptions. Their absence from the supramundane follows from the same rule: the path and fruition cittas take Nibbāna, and Nibbāna is not a being. They are absent from the great resultants for a parallel reason, since sense-sphere resultants take the object given them by past kamma rather than a being met with concern, and from the fifth jhāna and the immaterial attainments, which are grounded in equanimity rather than in the feeling these two require.

## Niyata and Aniyata

**Niyata — fixed adjuncts.** A factor is niyata in a given citta when it must arise whenever that citta arises. The seven universals are niyata everywhere; the four unwholesome universals are niyata in all twelve unwholesome cittas; the nineteen beautiful universals are niyata in all fifty-nine beautiful cittas; greed is niyata in the greed-rooted, hatred in the hatred-rooted, doubt in the doubt-accompanied citta, and wrong view in the four greed-rooted cittas classed as view-associated.

**Aniyata — unfixed adjuncts.** Eleven factors are aniyata: conceit, envy, stinginess, worry, sloth, torpor, the three abstinences, compassion, and appreciative joy. Each of these has a citta it can occur in, but it may or may not be present on a given occasion, so the saṅgaha totals for those cittas state a maximum rather than a fact.

The distinction is doing more than bookkeeping. It means a hatred-rooted citta is fully constituted without envy or worry, and that a great wholesome citta is fully wholesome without any abstinence or illimitable in it — those are additions, not requirements. The one place the distinction collapses is supramundane consciousness, path and fruition alike, where the three abstinences become fixed. That is the difference between an act of restraint and a nature that no longer transgresses.
